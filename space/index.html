Orbits in, numbers out

All three tools take the same orbit inputs: a preset, classical elements (perigee, apogee, inclination, node, argument of perigee and mean anomaly at a UTC epoch), or a pasted or uploaded two-line element set or OMM JSON record. Everything runs through the same SGP4/SDP4 propagator, so a TLE from CelesTrak and a hand-typed orbit behave the same way. Any orbit can be copied back out as a TLE. The debris and radiation tools export analysis as CSV. The star tracker exports raw detector images and attitude solutions. The radiation tool also encodes its cases in a shareable link.

Models and data

  • Catalog. A Space-Track.org snapshot of the public GP catalog retrieved October 23, 2026 (USSPACECOM / 18th & 19th Space Defense Squadrons), with CelesTrak SATCAT metadata. You can load a newer TLE or OMM file in the visualizer; it stays in your browser.
  • Trapped particles. NASA AE8/AP8 (NSSDC), tabulated with NASA GSFC's radbelt using the same cutoff-normalised interpolation as the models' own routine. Against direct model calls the median error is about 1%.
  • Geomagnetic coordinates. McIlwain L and B/B₀ from IGRF/DGRF on a geodetic grid, at the 1970 model epoch or the present-day field.
  • Dose. Continuous-slowing-down transport with NIST PSTAR/ESTAR tables. Proton dose matches CREME96 within 3% for the standard 1,000 km, 35° check case.
  • Star catalog. Hipparcos entries from HYG v4.1, through visual magnitude 7.5, with proper motion.
  • Small debris. Screening proxies for NASA ORDEM 3.1, ESA MASTER-8 and NASA MEM R2, digitised from Horstmann et al. (2021).

These are screening and education tools. They are not substitutes for AE9/AP9, SHIELDOSE-2, ORDEM 3.2 or conjunction data messages. Each tool's Method tab states its assumptions, validation and limits. Build scripts for every data table live in scripts/space.
