Orbit and star field

SGP4 propagates the selected TLE, OMM or reference elements. The scene uses a spherical Earth, approximate precession from the SGP4 frame to J2000, analytic solar and lunar ephemerides, and observer-position parallax for the Moon. Earth occults stars and creates a time-varying umbra and penumbra. HYG v4.1 supplies Hipparcos identifiers, Johnson V magnitudes and proper motions for 25,713 stars down to V = 7.5. Simulated rays include first-order annual and spacecraft aberration.

From photons to pixels

Stellar flux scales with aperture area, integration time and magnitude. An assumed visible passband and 45% combined transmission and quantum efficiency set the flux scale. Exposure subsamples capture slew and periodic jitter. The optical model includes a Gaussian point spread with off-axis broadening, radial distortion, vignetting, and empirical light scatter and ghosts. A 12,000-electron well and 96-count bias set the detector scale. Shot noise, fixed sensitivity variation, dark current, read noise, hot pixels, simplified blooming, and statistical particle hits alter the measured pixels.

From pixels to attitude

The tracker measures locally background-subtracted centroids, filters out peaks with poor shape, and converts pixel coordinates to calibrated camera rays. For blind acquisition it compares angular spacings against an indexed set of bright catalog pairs. It checks additional stars, rejects competing fits, and refines the rotation with a weighted least-squares fit to catalog directions. Tracking may reuse the previous frame’s solution. Simulation truth is used to report attitude error after the fit. The JSON export uses a scalar-first quaternion that rotates camera axes (right, down, forward) into the J2000-aligned inertial frame. Roll is positive clockwise from west toward south in the tangent plane.

Model limits

The default sensor is generic. Baffle rejection, limb brightness, surface glint, particle rate and the detector response are adjustable or illustrative approximations. A flight prediction needs measured point-source transmittance, detector calibration, material scattering, precise ephemerides and validation against hardware. Imported frames need the correct field of view, distortion and observation UTC. Inertial aberration correction also needs the observing orbit.
