Orbit and star field

SGP4 propagates the selected TLE, OMM or reference elements. The scene uses a spherical Earth, approximate precession from the SGP4 frame to J2000, analytic solar and lunar ephemerides, and observer-position parallax for the Moon. Earth occults stars and creates a time-varying umbra and penumbra. HYG v4.1 supplies Hipparcos identifiers, Johnson V magnitudes, B−V colors and proper motions for 25,713 stars down to V = 7.5. A seeded, galactic-latitude-weighted population adds uncatalogued stars from V = 7.5 to 10, which the tracker has to reject. Simulated rays include first-order annual and spacecraft aberration.

From photons to pixels

Stellar flux scales with aperture area, integration time and an instrument magnitude. Silicon detectors see red stars brighter than Johnson V does, so the instrument magnitude applies the Gaia G − V color relation to each star’s B−V. An assumed 8.8 × 10⁹ photons s⁻¹ m⁻² at magnitude zero and 45% combined transmission and quantum efficiency set the flux scale. Zodiacal light, shaped after the Leinert et al. (1998) tables, and diffuse galactic light set the sky background. Exposure subsamples capture slew, roll and periodic jitter. The optical model includes a pixel-integrated Gaussian point spread with off-axis broadening, radial distortion, vignetting, and empirical light scatter and ghosts. A 12,000-electron well and 96-count bias set the detector scale. Shot noise, fixed sensitivity variation, dark current, read noise, hot pixels and simplified blooming alter the measured pixels. Particle hits assume a 1 cm² detector behind about 4 mm of aluminum: galactic cosmic rays follow an L-shell rigidity cutoff, and AP8 solar-minimum protons above 30 MeV come from the IGRF 2025 field grid used by the Van Allen belt tool.

From pixels to attitude

The tracker measures locally background-subtracted centroids, refines each with an iteratively Gaussian-weighted window, filters out peaks with poor shape, and converts pixel coordinates to calibrated camera rays. For blind acquisition it compares angular spacings against an indexed set of bright catalog pairs in instrument magnitude. It checks additional stars, rejects competing fits, and refines the rotation by iteratively reweighted least squares. Each centroid is weighted by its SNR, catalog stars with a bright neighbor are flagged as blends, and a Cauchy loss suppresses outliers. The normal equations give a 1σ cross-boresight and roll uncertainty, scaled by the residual variance. Tracking propagates the previous solution with the body rate estimated from the last two frames. Simulation truth is used to report attitude error after the fit. The JSON export uses a scalar-first quaternion that rotates camera axes (right, down, forward) into the J2000-aligned inertial frame. Roll is positive clockwise from west toward south in the tangent plane.

Model limits

The default sensor is generic. Baffle rejection, limb brightness, surface glint, the faint-star population and the detector response are adjustable or illustrative approximations. The shutter is global, so rolling-shutter shear during fast slews is not modeled. A flight prediction needs measured point-source transmittance, detector calibration, material scattering, precise ephemerides and validation against hardware. Imported frames need the correct field of view, distortion and observation UTC. Inertial aberration correction also needs the observing orbit.
